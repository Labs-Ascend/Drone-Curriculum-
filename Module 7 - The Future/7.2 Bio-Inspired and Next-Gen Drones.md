# 7.2 Bio-Inspired and Next-Gen Drones

> [!abstract] Learning Objectives
> Study biomimetic flight, morphing wings, and biologically inspired autonomy.


### First Principles of Bio-Inspired Unmanned Aerial Systems

The next generation of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) relies on biomimetics to overcome the fundamental limitations of rigid-body rotorcraft. By imitating the morphology, kinematics, and fluid dynamics of biological organisms, engineers are solving complex challenges in aerodynamics, acoustic stealth, and physical robustness. Operating predominantly in the low Reynolds number regime ($Re \approx 30–1000$), insect and avian flight exploit unsteady aerodynamic mechanisms that are absent in steady-state, fixed-wing aviation . 

### Zoomorphic Designs and Unsteady Aerodynamics

Zoomorphic drones utilize flexible, flapping-wing architectures (ornithopters) to replicate the agile flight of birds and insects . Unlike conventional quadcopters, which rely on the brute-force generation of thrust via high-RPM propellers, zoomorphic designs leverage highly coupled multi-degree-of-freedom kinematics, including flap, twist, sweep, fold, and bend .

#### Aerodynamic Mechanisms
To generate lift at low Reynolds numbers, zoomorphic drones exploit three primary unsteady flow mechanisms:
*   **Delayed Stall (Leading-Edge Vortex):** During the translational phase of the wing stroke, a leading-edge vortex (LEV) forms and remains attached to the wing, creating a sustained low-pressure region that enhances instantaneous lift far beyond what steady-state aerodynamics predicts .
*   **Rotational Lift (Kramer Effect):** Rapid wing rotation at the end of a stroke creates bound circulation, generating forces that vectorially add to the translational lift .
*   **Wake Capture:** The wing interacts with the shed vortices of the previous stroke upon reversing direction, extracting kinetic energy from the fluid wake .

#### Fault Tolerance and Control
Zoomorphic designs also exhibit superior mechanical redundancy. For example, drones inspired by dragonflies can independently flap, twist, and curve four distinct wings . From a control systems perspective, if a quadcopter loses a rotor, catastrophic failure is imminent due to the loss of symmetric thrust and torque. Conversely, a dragonfly-inspired drone can compensate for a lost wing simply by dynamically increasing the flapping amplitude of the remaining wings . However, this highly coupled, non-linear flight regime introduces severe dynamic instability, requiring complex cascading proportional-integral-derivative (PID) or sliding-mode controllers to maintain attitude and position .

#### Human-Drone Interaction (HDI)
The morphological fidelity of a zoomorphic drone drastically alters human perception. While soft wings and biomimetic kinematics theoretically increase physical safety , imperfect biological mimicry triggers the "uncanny valley" effect, causing psychological discomfort [12-14]. Furthermore, the erratic, oscillating trajectory characteristic of flapping flight is often perceived by human observers as unstable or unsafe compared to the deterministic hover of a multi-rotor .

### Insect-Inspired Rotors for Aeroacoustic Stealth

Aeroacoustic noise is a primary bottleneck in drone deployment, consisting of tonal noise (periodic pressure fluctuations at the blade passage frequency) and broadband noise (stochastic fluctuations due to flow separation, vortex shedding, and blade-vortex interactions) . 

By analyzing the specific acoustic power of insects, researchers discovered that mosquitoes generate approximately 3.7 times more acoustic pressure per unit lift than fruit flies, despite operating at similar mass scales . Applying this biological insight to propeller design reveals a counter-intuitive first-principles strategy for noise reduction: increasing the rotor's planform area.

*   **Morphological Optimization:** By utilizing a blade shape inspired by the elliptical wings of fruit flies and increasing the total wetted area (up to 400% larger than standard rectangular blades), engineers can significantly alter the vortex shedding behavior .
*   **Kinematic Adjustment:** A larger blade area allows the rotor to generate equivalent mean lift at a drastically reduced non-dimensional rotation speed ($\tilde{\Omega} = \Omega/(\nu/R_o^2)$) . Because aeroacoustic power scales with the high power of the tip Mach number, reducing RPM geometrically diminishes acoustic emissions. 
*   **Vortex Dynamics (Q-Criterion):** Standard high-aspect-ratio rectangular blades shed complex, counter-rotating vortex pairs from the leading and trailing edges . In contrast, the enlarged, low-aspect-ratio bio-inspired blades consolidate the shedding into a single, stable rotating tip vortex . This minimizes the chaotic blade-vortex interactions that drive broadband noise, resulting in a quieter rotor that simultaneously requires less aerodynamic power per unit lift .

### Self-Righting Flapping Mechanics

A critical vulnerability of fixed-wing and bio-inspired MAVs is their inability to passively self-right after tipping over. Traditional robotics solutions—such as rigid roll cages, actuated levers, or heavy reconfigurable treads—add parasitic mass and aerodynamic drag, degrading the mass fraction and endurance of the drone .

Drawing inspiration from beetles (order Coleoptera), engineers have retrofitted fixed-wing drones with artificial *elytra* (hardened outer shell wings) to act as dynamic self-righting levers . This mechanism relies on fundamental rigid-body mechanics: generating torque ($\tau = r \times F$) around the drone's center of mass by pressing structural extensions against the ground.

#### Elytra Deployment Sequence
To right the inverted chassis, the drone executes a precise kinematic sequence utilizing two degrees of freedom (sweep and pitch):
1.  **Sweep Aft:** The elytra actuate 90° backward around their vertical axis, positioning their geometric center behind the drone's center of mass .
2.  **Pitch Up:** The elytra pitch downward against the ground by 180° . 
3.  **Torque Application:** Because the elytra extend significantly outward, the ground reaction force provides a massive mechanical advantage, levering the fuselage over its lateral axis into a metastable upright position .
4.  **Flight Configuration:** Once righted, the elytra reset to a 0° sweep and 0° pitch angle, preparing the MAV for takeoff .

Crucially, this bio-inspired mechanism avoids the severe energy penalty of traditional righting mechanisms. In flight, the artificial elytra function as lifting surfaces; the auxiliary aerodynamic lift they generate compensates for the added structural mass of the carbon fiber/Kevlar composite and the dual servo actuators .
